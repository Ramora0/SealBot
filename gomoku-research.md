# Minimal Recipe: Training a Gomoku NNUE from Scratch (with Optional AlphaZero Distillation)

## TL;DR
- **Build a value-only NNUE first**: a wide-sparse first layer ("accumulator") that is incrementally updated, followed by 2–3 tiny quantized layers with clipped-ReLU, feeding one scalar evaluation into your existing alpha-beta/PVS engine as a drop-in replacement for hand-crafted eval. For gomoku the natural feature set is board-square × stone-color (plus side-to-move) — the direct analog of chess's piece-square features, since there is no king and stones never move.
- **Train it on positions labeled by search-derived evaluations**, converting scores to win-probability via a sigmoid and fitting with MSE/cross-entropy, optionally blending in the game result (WDL) via a lambda interpolation. Data can come either from your minimax engine's own labeled self-play or (recommended, and what the state-of-the-art gomoku engine Rapfi actually does) from **distilling a strong AlphaZero-style teacher** ("Katagomo," a KataGo-derived gomoku engine).
- **Because gomoku has a large branching factor (~200 empty points early), the strongest real gomoku NNUE (Rapfi) adds a POLICY head** used inside alpha-beta for move ordering and dynamic depth adjustment — the key deviation from chess NNUEs, which are value-only. The addendum covers this precisely, drawing on Rapfi itself and on shogi practice.

## Key Findings

**1. The canonical reference is real and directly applicable.** The Stockfish `nnue-pytorch/docs/nnue.md` document is the authoritative, detailed description of NNUE architecture, quantization, and training. NNUE was invented for shogi by Yu Nasu, whose document "ƎUИИ Efficiently Updatable Neural-Network based Evaluation Functions for Computer Shogi" is dated April 28, 2018 (a promotional document for the 28th World Computer Shogi Championship, team "the end of genesis T.N.K.evolution turbo type D"). It was integrated into YaneuraOu by Motohiro Isozaki (May 2018), and ported to chess in Stockfish by Hisayori "nodchip" Noda (2019–2020; the Stockfish 12 merge credits "nodchip, ynasu87, yaneurao (initial port and NNUE authors)"). The gain was large: Stockfish's official "Introducing NNUE Evaluation" blog (Aug 6, 2020) reported ">80 Elo on fishtest" (a measured run showed "ELO: 92.77 +-2.1 (95%), Total: 60000" at 10+0.1, 1 thread), and Chess.com reported "more than 100 Elo points of improvement relative to Stockfish 11 ... approximately two years of traditional improvement in the span of a month."

**2. There is a purpose-built, award-winning gomoku NNUE that answers your question end-to-end.** Rapfi (Jin, Duan, Hang; arXiv 2503.13178, "Rapfi: Distilling Efficient Neural Network for the Game of Gomoku") uses an NNUE-style network ("Mixnet") with an incremental-update accumulator, alpha-beta/PVS search, BOTH value and policy heads, and **distillation from an AlphaZero-style teacher**. Per the paper's abstract, "Rapfi reached strength surpassing Katagomo, the strongest open-source Gomoku AI based on AlphaZero's algorithm, under limited computational resources where accelerators like GPUs are absent," and "Rapfi ranks #1 among 520 Gomoku agents on Botzone, and won the championship, defeating 54 competitors in the world's largest Gomoku/Renju AI competition" (GomoCup 2024). This is the single most important source for a gomoku-specific recipe.

**3. Value-only vs value+policy is the central design fork.** Chess NNUEs (Stockfish and derivatives) are value-only; move ordering is handled by classical heuristics (history, killers, SEE, LMR). AlphaZero/MCTS engines (Lc0, KataGo, dlshogi) use both value and policy. Gomoku sits in between: its large branching factor makes a policy head valuable for taming the tree, which is exactly why Rapfi includes one even though it uses alpha-beta rather than MCTS.

## Details

### QUESTION 1 — TRUNK (BACKBONE) ARCHITECTURE

**Standard NNUE architecture (as in Stockfish).** An NNUE is a shallow, quantized MLP with an unusual first layer:

- **Feature transformer / first accumulator layer.** The input is an enormous but extremely sparse binary feature vector (tens of thousands of features; input sparsity on the order of 0.1%). The first layer multiplies this by a weight matrix to produce the "accumulator" — the first hidden layer's pre-activation values. Because inputs are binary and sparse, this is computed as a sum of weight columns for the active features plus a bias. Stockfish maintains TWO accumulator "perspectives," one from each side to move, concatenated.
- **Layer sizes.** Historically `256x2→32→32→1`; later `512x2→16→32→1`; modern Stockfish nets use much wider accumulators — commonly 1024, and some top engines up to 3072 per perspective — with narrow later layers (~16/32 wide). The design principle: "wide and shallow" — nearly all knowledge lives in the giant first layer; later layers are kept tiny because extra depth adds little accuracy and accumulates quantization error.
- **Activations.** Clipped ReLU: `clamp(x, 0, 127)` in the quantized domain (`min(max(x,0),1)` in float). Modern Stockfish also uses squared clipped ReLU (SCReLU) on the accumulator output for a bit more accuracy. Clipping (not plain ReLU) is essential because aggressive quantization needs bounded activation ranges.
- **Quantization.** Weights and activations are integers. The feature-transformer/accumulator is kept in int16 (to avoid overflow during incremental add/subtract); later linear layers use int8 weights with int32 dot-product accumulation, then rescale. This gives ~4× smaller models and lets modern CPU SIMD (AVX2/AVX-512 VNNI, NEON) evaluate millions of positions/sec. Quantization error is negligible in such shallow nets.
- **The "efficiently updatable" property.** Between two consecutive search positions (a make/unmake move), only a handful of input features change. Instead of recomputing the accumulator from scratch (O(features × width)), you add the weight columns for newly-activated features and subtract those for deactivated ones — O(changed features × width). This is the whole point: it makes a neural eval cheap enough to call at every node of an alpha-beta tree. Only the first layer is efficiently updatable; once the nonlinearity is applied, the later (tiny) layers are recomputed fresh each node. Accumulators are stored on the search stack per ply so unmake is free.
- **Feature set (chess).** HalfKP: each active feature is a tuple `(our_king_square, piece_square, piece_type, piece_color)` for non-king pieces, giving one sparse index per piece, indexed relative to the king. HalfKA variants include the king. The takeaway from the docs: simple feature sets (HalfKP-like) win because they are cheap to update and change little per move; elaborate domain-specific features usually don't pay for their speed cost.

**Adapting the trunk to gomoku.** Gomoku has no king, pieces never move, and a move only ever ADDS one stone (or, in unmake, removes one). This is the *ideal* case for NNUE:

- **Input features (the "A"/all-pieces analog).** The natural minimal feature set is board-square × stone-color: for a 15×15 board, `225 squares × 2 colors = 450` binary features (a stone of a given color on a given point), plus side-to-move handling (two perspectives, or a side-to-move plane). This is the direct gomoku analog of chess's simplest "A" (all-pieces / piece-square) features described in the Stockfish docs. There is no king, so there is no HalfK-style king-indexing; you simply drop that dimension.
- **Incremental update is even cheaper than chess.** A gomoku move changes exactly ONE feature (one stone appears). So each accumulator update is a single vector add (and unmake is a single subtract) — the minimal possible. This is why NNUE is a spectacular fit for gomoku alpha-beta search.
- **What Rapfi actually does (a stronger, gomoku-tuned alternative to plain square features).** Rapfi's Mixnet does NOT feed raw per-square one-hot features into a dense first layer. Instead it exploits the fact that gomoku is about *lines*: it decomposes the board into four directional line-patterns (horizontal, vertical, two diagonals) through each point, each of length 11 (covering the 5-in-a-row window in both directions). A "mapping network" (a small directional-convolution CNN with five Dir-Conv layers) converts each local line pattern into a feature vector; after training this mapping network is "baked" losslessly into a **pattern-indexed codebook** (a lookup table of N = 397,488 possible line patterns → C-channel feature). At inference the per-point feature is just four table lookups (one per direction) summed and passed through ReLU, then a depth-wise 3×3 conv over half the channels. This codebook + accumulator plays exactly the role of NNUE's feature transformer, and it is incrementally updatable: placing one stone affects at most 4×11 line features, so only those are recomputed and the accumulator for the processed feature map is updated by adding delta activations.
- **Minimal recommendation.** For a *minimal* recipe that captures most of the gain: start with plain `board-square × color` (450-feature) transformer into a wide accumulator (say 256–1024), clipped-ReLU, then `→32→1`, int16 accumulator / int8 later layers. This is the smallest thing that works and integrates with your engine. If you want gomoku state-of-the-art, adopt Rapfi's line-pattern codebook trunk, which is empirically far stronger per FLOP because it captures the line structure that matters in five-in-a-row. Rapfi quantizes its pattern-based codebook features to a small integer range (`[−16, 16]`).

### QUESTION 2 — HEADS AND SEARCH CONSUMPTION

**What chess NNUEs do (value-only).** Stockfish's NNUE has a single scalar output head: a position evaluation. The search (alpha-beta with Principal Variation Search) calls it as the leaf/interior evaluation function, replacing the old hand-crafted eval. Move ordering, pruning, and reductions are done by classical heuristics (transposition-table move, killers, counter-moves, history/continuation history, SEE, futility, null-move, LMR). There is NO policy head in Stockfish. This is the value-only integration pattern: `eval = NNUE(position)`, everything else is search machinery.

**How a value-only NNUE integrates with alpha-beta/PVS/minimax.** It is a direct drop-in for your existing evaluation function. At each node where you would have called `hand_crafted_eval(pos)`, you instead read the incrementally-maintained accumulator, run the tiny remaining layers, rescale (Stockfish divides the output layer by `FV_SCALE = 16` and adds small adjustments), and return the score in your engine's internal units. Alpha-beta, PVS zero-window scouts, transposition table, and pruning are unchanged. The only integration work is: (a) maintain the accumulator on make/unmake, and (b) refresh it fully at the root or when you can't incrementally reach a clean state.

**Whether/how a policy head helps, and what the best gomoku engine does.** A policy head outputs a probability/score per legal move (for gomoku, per empty point: `π ∈ R^(H×W)`). In MCTS engines it drives PUCT selection. In alpha-beta it is NOT needed for correctness, but it is valuable for *efficiency* when the branching factor is large — and gomoku's is large (~200+ legal moves in the opening on 15×15). Rapfi, the strongest gomoku engine of this type, includes a policy head AND uses alpha-beta/PVS. Per its paper (Appendix A.4), it "leverage[s] neural network policy predictions to rank moves and adjust search depth dynamically" — i.e., the policy is used for (1) **move ordering** (rank candidate moves so cutoffs happen early) and (2) **dynamic depth/reduction decisions** (LMR-style: search policy-unlikely moves less deeply). Rapfi *also* supports a pure-MCTS/PUCT mode that uses policy + value the AlphaZero way, but its competition-winning configuration is alpha-beta/PVS with the policy assisting move ordering and reductions, layered on top of a classical candidate-move system (only empty points near existing stones are candidates) and pattern-based move scores.

**Bottom line for heads.** A minimal value-only NNUE is sufficient to strengthen an existing minimax engine and is the smallest recipe. But for gomoku specifically, adding a policy head is the single most impactful "big" extra technique because it directly attacks the large branching factor — and it is exactly what the state-of-the-art gomoku engine does. See the addendum for the full three-question treatment of the policy-including design.

### QUESTION 3 — LOSSES

**Score-to-win-probability conversion.** NNUE training works in "WDL space" (0 = loss, 0.5 = draw, 1 = win) rather than raw centipawn/score space. A search-derived score is mapped through a sigmoid: `wdl = sigmoid(score / scaling_factor)` (Stockfish's pytorch trainer uses `scaling_factor ≈ 410`; the earlier C++ learner used `1/(1+10^(-eval/4)) = sigmoid(eval/4·ln10)`). Working in WDL space keeps gradients well-behaved and makes large evals "closer together," matching the fact that huge advantages need not be precisely valued.

**The core loss.** Fit the network's (sigmoid-converted) output to the target in WDL space. The simplest is MSE; cross-entropy is also standard and is what Stockfish's C++ learner used (`test_cross_entropy`). Both work; MSE is the minimal choice.

**WDL / game-result blending (lambda interpolation).** The training target blends the search-derived evaluation with the actual game outcome:

`target = λ · sigmoid(score/K) + (1−λ) · game_result`

(equivalently the loss itself can be blended: `loss = λ·loss_eval + (1−λ)·loss_result`). λ = 1.0 uses only the engine's score; λ = 0.0 uses only the game result; intermediate values mix them. Stockfish's `--lambda` parameter does exactly this ("lambda of 1.0 means use purely the score ... 0.0 means to use purely the outcome"). In shogi (nodchip/YaneuraOu) recipes, common values are λ = 1.0 for a bootstrap phase and λ = 0.5 (or 0.33) for later phases mixing in outcomes.

**Policy loss (only if you have a policy head).** Cross-entropy between the predicted policy distribution and a target distribution. The target is the teacher/search visit-count distribution (MCTS π) or, in distillation, the teacher network's policy.

**Rapfi's actual loss (the gomoku-specific answer).** Rapfi trains Mixnet with cross-entropy loss on both a categorical value target `V = (p_win, p_loss, p_draw) ∈ R^3` and a normalized policy target `π ∈ R^(H×W)`, using **knowledge distillation** from a ResNet teacher (Resnet-6b128f) pretrained on the same data. Crucially, it uses a **mixed loss: 75% weight on the distillation labels (teacher's soft outputs) and 25% on the true labels** (game result / recorded policy). Optimizer: Adam (lr = 0.001, β1 = 0.9, β2 = 0.999, ε = 1e−8), batch size 128, ~600k iterations.

### HOW THE TOP NNUEs ARE TRAINED (data pipeline)

**Stockfish — self-play, evaluation-labeled.** Yes, the data is generated by self-play. Stockfish has a `generate_training_data` tool: it plays fast self-play games from an opening book (e.g., `noob_3moves.epd`), and for each position records a *search-derived evaluation label* at a fixed low depth (e.g., depth 9) or fixed node count (e.g., 5000 nodes), often with `random_multi_pv` for diversity. A recent good dataset was ~16 billion positions; datasets have reached 64B positions. The historical transition: the *first* NNUEs were trained on positions labeled by Stockfish's **classical hand-crafted evaluation** at moderate depth (pure distillation of the old eval into a net — effectively a "search-depth multiplier"). Once NNUE beat the classical eval, subsequent nets were trained on data labeled by **NNUE-based self-play** (the net labels its own successor's data), plus mixing in Leela (Lc0) self-play data. So the pipeline evolved from HCE-labeled → NNUE-self-play-labeled, an iterative reinforcement-flavored loop.

**Contrast with Lc0/AlphaZero.** Leela/AlphaZero nets are trained by pure reinforcement learning from self-play, with the value head fit to game outcomes and the policy head fit to MCTS visit counts. These are the "teachers" you would distill from.

### DISTILLATION FROM AN ALPHAZERO-STYLE TEACHER (optional, but recommended for gomoku)

Distillation means training your small fast NNUE to reproduce a strong slow teacher's outputs as *soft targets*:
- **Value distillation:** teacher's value (win/draw/loss probabilities or scalar win-prob) becomes the value target for your NNUE — more informative than the raw game result because it is a smooth, calibrated estimate.
- **Policy distillation (if you have a policy head):** teacher's policy distribution becomes the cross-entropy target for your policy head.
- **Blend with true labels:** use a mix (Rapfi: 75% teacher soft labels + 25% true labels).

This is exactly Rapfi's approach: the teacher is Katagomo (a KataGo-derived AlphaZero-style gomoku engine) which generated ~30.8 million self-play positions through an AlphaZero-like self-play process over several weeks, each a tuple `(board, value_target, policy_target)`; Mixnet is then distilled from a ResNet teacher trained on that data. **KataGo's teacher architecture** is a convolutional residual trunk (model sizes such as b6c128 = 6 blocks × 128 channels up to b20c256 and larger nested-bottleneck variants) with a policy head and a value head (plus auxiliary ownership/score heads), trained by AlphaZero-style self-play with the value head predicting the game outcome and the policy head mimicking MCTS visit counts. Distillation is optional — you can train purely on your minimax engine's labeled self-play — but distilling from a top AlphaZero-style net captures the most performance for the least data and is what the winning gomoku engine does.

### DATA GENERATION — CLEAR STATEMENT

Two viable approaches; pick based on resources:
1. **Minimax-labeled self-play (no teacher).** Use your existing alpha-beta engine to play fast self-play games from balanced openings; label each position with the engine's fixed-depth/fixed-node search score; convert to WDL and train with lambda-blended MSE/CE against score and game result. This is the Stockfish bootstrap path and needs no external net.
2. **Distillation from an AlphaZero-style teacher (recommended for gomoku).** Generate positions from teacher self-play (or your own games), label with the teacher's value (and policy if using a policy head), train with a mixed distillation/true-label loss. This is Rapfi's path and the fastest route to top strength.

## ADDENDUM — The Most Similar Engine That Includes a Policy in Its Fast Eval for Large Branching Factors

The task asks: if the minimal recipe is value-only (no policy for move ordering/pruning), provide an addendum from the most similar engine that DOES include a policy in its NNUE/NNUE-like fast net for handling large state spaces. **The single best analog is Rapfi itself** — it is a gomoku engine (same game), uses an NNUE-style incrementally-updatable net, runs alpha-beta/PVS, AND includes a policy head specifically because gomoku's branching factor is large. I also cover shogi practice, since shogi is the classic large-branching-factor NNUE domain.

### Rapfi (gomoku) — the same three questions

**Trunk architecture.** As above: a one-hot `2×H×W` board is decomposed into four directional line patterns (length 11) per point; a mapping-network CNN (five Dir-Conv layers with interleaved 1×1 convs and skip connections) converts patterns to C-channel features, baked into a pattern-indexed codebook (N = 397,488 entries) for O(1) lookup; features from four directions are summed + ReLU'd; a depth-wise 3×3 conv over half the channels enlarges the receptive field. This whole feature map is incrementally updatable (one stone ⇒ ≤4×11 line features change), with an accumulator holding the processed feature map. Three sizes were tested (mapping channels M / feature channels C): Small (M=64, C=32; P=16, V=32 for the heads), Medium, and Large, each doubling channels. Codebook features are quantized to `[−16, 16]`.

**Heads and search consumption.** Two heads on the shared feature map:
- **Value head:** predicts categorical WDL `v ∈ R^3`, using "value grouping" (3×3 spatial chunks pooled) and "star blocks" (a multiplicative/kernel-trick nonlinearity) then a three-layer MLP.
- **Policy head:** predicts `π ∈ R^(H×W)` via a "dynamic policy convolution" — global average pooling produces weights/bias for a point-wise conv over the first P channels, letting the policy adapt to global board context.

The search consumes them two ways. In its **alpha-beta/PVS** competition mode: the value head is the leaf/interior evaluation; the policy head is used to "rank moves and adjust search depth dynamically" (move ordering + LMR-style reductions), on top of a classical candidate system (only empty points within a configured distance of existing stones are legal candidates, governed by a `CandidateRange` setting) and pattern-based move-ordering scores. A VCF (Victory-by-Continuous-Fours) search runs at leaves like quiescence search, plus futility pruning, null-move pruning, LMR, singular extensions, and a transposition table, all SPSA-tuned. In its alternative **MCTS/PUCT** mode: policy + value are used the AlphaZero way. Ablations show the policy head matters far more in MCTS (removing dynamic policy conv costs >100 Elo) than in alpha-beta (nearly neutral), confirming that in alpha-beta the policy is a helpful-but-secondary ordering/reduction signal, while the value head carries the load.

**Losses.** Cross-entropy on both value (categorical WDL) and policy (vs teacher/recorded policy distribution), with knowledge distillation: mixed loss of 75% teacher soft labels + 25% true labels. Adam, lr 0.001, batch 128, ~600k iters. Teacher = ResNet (6 blocks, 128 filters) trained on Katagomo's ~30.8M self-play positions.

### Shogi practice (the classic large-branching-factor NNUE domain)

Shogi has a branching factor of roughly 80–100 (drops make it large). Two families:
- **Alpha-beta + NNUE (YaneuraOu, Suisho, tanuki-, Kristallweizen):** These are **value-only** NNUE, using a HalfKP-style feature set (extended for shogi's hand/drop pieces) and the same `256x2→32→32→1`-style architecture ported from/to Stockfish. They tame the huge branching factor NOT with a neural policy but with classical Stockfish-style move ordering (transposition move, SEE captures, killers, counter-moves, history/continuation history) plus aggressive forward pruning (futility, null-move, LMR — the Hoki–Muramatsu shogi techniques). This shows a value-only NNUE *can* handle a large branching factor via search heuristics alone.
- **MCTS + policy/value (dlshogi, AobaZero):** AlphaZero-style; a policy network narrows search via PUCT priors and a value network evaluates leaves. Here the policy is consumed as a prior probability in tree selection, not as move ordering in a depth-first tree.

The instructive contrast: Rapfi is unusual and directly on-point because it takes an AlphaZero-style *policy head* (like dlshogi) but consumes it inside a Stockfish/YaneuraOu-style *alpha-beta/PVS* search — using the policy to rank moves and drive dynamic depth, which is precisely the technique for handling a large branching factor in a depth-first NNUE engine.

## Recommendations

**Stage 0 — decide the head set.** If your only goal is to beat your current hand-crafted eval with minimal work: build a **value-only NNUE** (Stockfish pattern). If you want gomoku state-of-the-art and can afford one extra head: build **value + policy** (Rapfi pattern). Given gomoku's large branching factor, I recommend value+policy — the policy head is the highest-leverage "big" technique here.

**Stage 1 — trunk.** Minimal: `450 (square×color) → accumulator (256–1024) → clipped-ReLU → 32 → 1`, int16 accumulator, int8 later layers, incremental single-stone updates. Stronger (recommended for gomoku): adopt Rapfi's directional line-pattern codebook trunk. Threshold to upgrade: if per-FLOP strength or search speed is inadequate with plain square features, switch to the line-pattern codebook.

**Stage 2 — data.** Minimal/no-teacher: generate self-play from your alpha-beta engine, label positions with fixed-depth (~depth 8–10) or fixed-node (~5000-node) search scores, from balanced openings, tens to hundreds of millions of positions. Recommended: distill from an AlphaZero-style gomoku teacher (KataGo-for-gomoku / Katagomo); Rapfi used ~30.8M teacher-labeled positions with (board, WDL, policy) tuples.

**Stage 3 — losses.** Convert scores with `sigmoid(score/K)`. Train value with MSE or cross-entropy in WDL space, blending game result via lambda (start λ=1.0 to bootstrap from scores, then λ≈0.5 to mix in outcomes). If distilling, use a mixed loss (~75% teacher soft labels + 25% true labels, per Rapfi). If you have a policy head, add cross-entropy vs teacher/visit-count policy.

**Stage 4 — search integration.** Maintain the accumulator on make/unmake; refresh at root. Replace your leaf eval with the value head (rescaled). If you have a policy head, use it for move ordering and LMR-style reductions; keep a VCF/threat quiescence search at leaves (the gomoku analog of chess quiescence) — this is a big tactical win in five-in-a-row. Tune pruning parameters (SPSA if possible).

**Benchmarks that change the plan.** If value-only already dominates your old eval and search speed is the bottleneck, don't add the policy head. If you observe the search exploding on branching factor (nodes/move very high, shallow depth), add/strengthen the policy head and candidate-move restriction. If distillation data is unavailable, fall back to minimax-labeled self-play with λ blending.

## Caveats
- **The exact policy-consumption constants in Rapfi (top-k move cap, policy threshold, precise policy→reduction formula) are not published in the paper** and live in the engine's `search/movepick.cpp` / `search/ab/search.cpp` source; the paper states only that policy is used "to rank moves and adjust search depth dynamically." Treat any specific top-k number as an implementation detail to read from source, not an established value.
- **Rapfi's competition-winning trunk is the line-pattern codebook, not a plain square-feature transformer.** The plain-square recipe is the minimal illustrative version; it will be weaker per FLOP than Rapfi's design. Both are legitimate NNUE architectures.
- **Layer sizes for chess evolve rapidly** (256→512→1024→3072 accumulators); specific numbers cited are period-accurate but not fixed. For gomoku, no single "standard" size exists; Rapfi's sizes (M=64+, C=32+) are far smaller than chess accumulators because the codebook front-end does the heavy lifting.
- **"Efficiently updatable" strictly applies only to the first layer.** Later layers are recomputed each node; keep them tiny.
- Some cited figures (Elo deltas, position counts) come from engine test logs and papers and reflect specific hardware/time-control conditions; treat them as indicative, not universal.
- **KataGo is not strictly an NNUE** (it's a GPU ResNet with full recompute per position, not incremental). It is cited as the *teacher* architecture and the conceptual source of the value+policy+auxiliary-head design, not as the fast CPU net itself.
